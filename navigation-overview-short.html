Navigation guide

Where to go for what

Published

November 18, 2026

New here? Start with the Onboarding page (about 15 minutes), then come back to find your way. This page is a map of the documentation: what is where and how the pages relate to each other. It does not repeat the content.

TipThe one-paragraph version

The documentation helps you to choose the simplest setup that meets your current needs: a hosted service first, then Docker and Docker Compose, and Kubernetes when you need scale and availability. Installing by hand (virtual machine, bare metal) is the fallback when no usable image exists. Storage, backups, security, data protection, assemblies of several tools, semantic graphs and LLMs are covered in separate pages that point to each other.

Site map

Every box is a page of the site and can be clicked. Solid arrows are direct links between pages. Dashed arrows are links to outside resources or thematic relations.

The complexity ladder

Choose the simplest setup that meets your needs, and check first whether a hosted service already exists. When a ready-made image is available, Docker and then Docker Compose are the easiest ways to set a platform up, and Kubernetes is the step up when you need scale and availability. Read the blue line from left to right as growing scale. The pink box is about something else: installing by hand, which is the fallback when no usable image exists, and is more work to install, update and maintain. Every box links to its section of the step-by-step guide.

NoteWhat applies at every step

Linux basics, scalability, databases, security, the Backups and Security page, automation, and the choice of tools are not tied to one step. Treat them as a layer under the whole ladder.

The journey of the data across the pages

Six pages deal with the data itself, not with the infrastructure. They follow a natural order, from where the data lives to how it is described, queried and assisted by an LLM.
